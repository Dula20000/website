Solid rocket motor internal ballistics with level-set grain regression: thrust-curve shaping and Monte Carlo pressure dispersion

Dulran · September 2026

fast-marching level set lumped ballistics · ODE + quasi-steady KNSB · APCP · SRB-class Monte Carlo MEOP interactive solver ↓ thrust curves (CSV)

Abstract

I built a solid rocket motor (SRM) design tool that regresses an arbitrary two-dimensional grain cross-section with a second-order fast-marching level-set method, assembles the burning area of a segmented grain, and solves the lumped chamber ballistics both in the quasi-steady equilibrium form and as a transient mass-balance ODE. The circular-port perimeter converges at order to the exact \(2\pi(r_0+w)\); star grains converge at first order because of their corners. The solver reproduces the four textbook thrust-curve shapes at equal propellant mass, recovers the BATES rule \(L\approx(3D+d)/2\), and qualitatively reproduces the two-hump "thrust bucket" of an SRB-class booster. For a 98 mm-class APCP motor (,  N·s), a -sample Monte Carlo over burn-rate coefficient, exponent, throat diameter and density gives a 99.865th-percentile peak pressure (the 3σ-equivalent MEOP) of  MPa, × the nominal peak. Every tolerance is amplified by \(1/(1-n)\), and the exponent's own uncertainty is the largest single contributor to the spread. 1Motivation

A solid motor has no throttle: once the grain is cast, the thrust history is fixed by how its burning surface evolves. The designer shapes that history through the port geometry, which is why grain design is the core of SRM work, from amateur KNSB motors to the Space Shuttle boosters whose forward star segment was shaped to lower thrust through the region of maximum dynamic pressure. The same equilibrium that sets the pressure level also makes it sensitive: with \(P_c\propto K_n^{1/(1-n)}\), a small error in burn rate or throat area becomes a larger error in pressure, and the case has to be designed for the high tail of that spread, not for the nominal.

This study builds the tool chain end to end: a general grain-regression solver (not a table of closed-form shapes), a ballistics model checked against its own equilibrium limit, and a dispersion analysis that turns tolerances into a maximum expected operating pressure (MEOP).

2Method

2.1 Grain regression by fast marching

Each axial segment has a 2-D cross-section of outer radius \(R\) (outer surface bonded and inhibited). The port is an implicit function \(\phi_0(x,y)\): negative in the gas, positive in the propellant, and a signed distance near the surface. With the burn rate uniform over the cross-section, the burning surface after web \(w\) has burned is a level set of the arrival-time field \(T\):

$$|\nabla T| = 1 \ \text{in the propellant},\qquad T=\phi_0 \ \text{on the initial surface},\qquad \Gamma(w)=\{T=w\}.$$

\(T\) is computed once per geometry with Sethian's fast-marching method, using second-order one-sided differences where two accepted upwind nodes exist. The nodes within three cells of the surface are initialised from \(\phi_0\) (narrow-band initialisation), which removes most of the first-order error the march otherwise picks up at sharp star tips. The burning perimeter \(P(w)\) is the length of the marching-squares contour \(T=w\), clipped to the case circle. The port area follows from the co-area identity \(A_p(w)=A_p(0)+\int_0^w P\,dw'\), which keeps \(A_b = -dV_\text{prop}/dw\) exactly consistent. Primitives are a circular BATES core, an \(N\)-point star polygon, a finocyl-style slotted core (circle ∪ radial slots) and an offset moon-burner core. Unions use \(\min(\phi_i)\), which is exact on the propellant side, the only side that is marched.

For a segment of length \(L_k\) with \(e_k\in\{0,1,2\}\) uninhibited ends, a point is burned when \(\min(T, z)\le w\), where \(z\) is its distance to an exposed end. The burning area is therefore

$$A_b(w)=\sum_k\Big[P_k(w)\,\big(L_k-e_k w\big)+e_k\big(\pi R^2-A_{p,k}(w)\big)\Big].$$

2.2 Chamber ballistics

The burn rate follows Saint-Robert/Vieille, \(r=a\,(P_c/1\,\text{MPa})^n\), piecewise in pressure where the propellant data require it. The lumped chamber of free volume \(V\) (case volume minus remaining propellant) obeys the gas mass balance with an ideal nozzle:

$$\begin{gathered}\frac{V}{RT_0}\frac{dP_c}{dt}=(\rho_p-\rho_g)\,A_b\,r-\dot m_n,\qquad \frac{dw}{dt}=r,\\ \dot m_n=\frac{P_cA_t}{c^*}\ \text{(choked)},\qquad RT_0=(c^*\Gamma)^2 .\end{gathered}$$

It is integrated with RK4 from ambient pressure (whole surface ignited at \(t=0\), no igniter mass), with a step of 0.2 of the chamber filling time \(Vc^*/(RT_0A_t)\) and no more than half a table spacing in \(w\). Below the subsonic-exit pressure ratio the throat unchokes and the flow is computed isentropically to ambient. Dropping the storage term and \(\rho_g\) gives the quasi-steady equilibrium used for comparison and for the Monte Carlo:

$$P_c=\left(\frac{\rho_p\,a\,c^*\,K_n}{P_\text{ref}^{\,n}}\right)^{\!1/(1-n)},\qquad K_n=\frac{A_b}{A_t},\qquad t(w)=\int_0^w\frac{dw'}{r(P_c)}.$$

Thrust is \(F=C_F P_c A_t\) with the ideal thrust coefficient for the exit-to-throat area ratio \(\varepsilon\) and ambient pressure \(p_a\) (Sutton & Biblarz):

$$C_F=\sqrt{\frac{2\gamma^2}{\gamma-1}\Big(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\Big)^{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}}\Big[1-\Big(\frac{p_e}{P_c}\Big)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\Big]}+\frac{p_e-p_a}{P_c}\,\varepsilon .$$

Two optional effects are available as switches, both off unless stated: throat erosion (constant recession rate of the throat diameter) and erosive burning by Lenoir–Robillard, \(r=r_0+\alpha G^{0.8}D_h^{-0.2}e^{-\beta r_0\rho_p/G}\), evaluated per axial segment from the mass flux \(G\) generated upstream.

2.3 Motor metrics

Total impulse \(I=\int F\,dt\) over the whole trace. Burn (action) time: from the first to the last instant at which \(F\ge 10\,\%\,F_\text{max}\); certification bodies use their own definitions. Average thrust \(=I/t_b\). \(I_{sp}=I/(m_p g_0)\). Class letter by total impulse (A: 1.26–2.5 N·s, each letter doubles, as used by NAR/TRA). Neutrality: \(P_{c,\max}/P_{c,\min}\) of the equilibrium pressure over the web burn, taken as \(0\le w\le 0.97\,w_\text{web}\), where \(w_\text{web}\) is the first contact of the flame front with the case (or the end-burn limit \(L/e\)). Shape ratio: mean thrust over the last third of the burn time divided by the first third.

3Verification & validation

Validation leans on exact solutions I can compute myself. For a circular port the regressed perimeter is exactly \(2\pi(r_0+w)\). For a star polygon, the outward offset is exact until the first edge is consumed at a valley: \(P(w)=P_0+w\sum_\text{tips}\theta_i-2w\sum_\text{valleys}\tan(|\theta_j|/2)\), with \(\theta\) the turning angle at each vertex. BATES grains have the closed form \(A_b=\pi(d+2w)(L-2w)+\tfrac{\pi}{2}\big(D^2-(d+2w)^2\big)\).

The first-order behaviour for stars is a property of the geometry, not the march: contouring the exact distance function converges at the same rate, because the level sets keep a corner at each valley until the valley edges are consumed. The same corners make the raw co-area closure \(A_p(0)+\int P\,dw\) fall short of \(\pi R^2\) by about for the eight-point star at N = 161, halving with each grid doubling. The tool rescales \(P(w)\) by that closure so that the burned mass equals the cast propellant mass exactly; the correction is below \(10^{-5}\) for circular ports.

The transient ODE settles onto the equilibrium solution during the web burn. At mid-web the equilibrium pressure is higher than the ODE by (KNSB) and (APCP). That offset is the gas-density term \(\rho_g/\rho_p\) that the equilibrium formula drops, amplified by \(1/(1-n)\), and it is larger for KNSB because its local exponent above 3.8 MPa is 0.535. Total impulse agrees to and , because the filling and tail-off transients carry little impulse. Reducing the ODE step fourfold changes impulse and peak pressure by less than \(10^{-3}\).

The rule follows from \(dA_b/dw = 2\pi(L-2d-6w)\), which is zero at mid-web and gives equal initial and final areas when \(L=(3D+d)/2\). The rule implies – segments across the core ratios studied, and for core ratios the most neutral integer count equals the nearest integer to that value. Larger cores are more neutral because the shorter web limits how far \(A_b\) can drift, but they carry less propellant: kg at \(d/D=0.55\) vs kg at 0.20 (at fixed total length the mass does not depend on N). When \(L/2\) approaches the radial web, the grain also ends as a thin, fast-shrinking disc.

The peak-pressure distribution is right-skewed. The ratio of lognormals that \(P_c^{1/(1-n)}\) produces puts the empirical 99.865th percentile, MPa, above the normal-theory μ + 3σ of MPa. Using μ + 3σ would understate the MEOP. With an assumed ultimate factor of safety of (a common choice; the governing standard sets the real value), the case must hold MPa, a design factor of on the nominal peak. Total impulse varies far less (σ/μ = ), since it scales with propellant mass and only weakly with pressure through \(C_F\).

Why n dominates. Linearising the equilibrium pressure gives

$$\delta\ln P_c=\frac{1}{1-n}\Big[\delta\ln a+\delta\ln\rho_p-2\,\delta\ln D_t+\ln\!\Big(\frac{P_c}{P_\text{ref}}\Big)\delta n\Big].$$

Two things follow. First, the \(1/(1-n)\) factor ( here) multiplies every tolerance, so the nominal exponent sets how hard the whole tolerance stack is amplified. Figure 11 shows the 3σ-equivalent MEOP factor growing from at n = 0.20 to at n = 0.70 with the tolerances unchanged. Second, the exponent's own uncertainty acts through a lever arm \(\ln(P_c/P_\text{ref}) = \) . At a 5 MPa operating point and σn = , that is a effective rate error against the from \(a\), which makes n the largest single contributor ( % of the variance). Figure 10b makes a caveat plain: that lever arm exists only because \(a\) is referenced to 1 MPa. If the same tolerances are defined at the operating pressure, as a strand-burner fit centred on \(P_c\) would give, the n contribution nearly vanishes at the peak and the spread drops to σ/μ = from . So how the burn-rate data are fitted and quoted is itself a design variable for MEOP.

7Limitations & future work

  • Lumped (0-D) chamber. No axial pressure drop, flow in the port, or head-end to aft-end pressure ratio. The erosive switch uses a per-segment mass flux from the upstream generation, which is only a proxy. A 1-D quasi-steady port-flow model is the natural next step, and is needed for low port-to-throat ratios like the KNSB motor's .
  • 2-D regression per segment. Uniform burn rate over each cross-section. Tapered bores, 3-D fins and conocyl grains need a 3-D level set, and the SRB-class case fakes a taper with steps.
  • Grid accuracy for cornered ports. Perimeter errors converge only at first order for stars and slots, and the mass-conserving rescale spreads a local error over the burn. Corner-aware (subcell) initialisation or finer grids would help. The browser grid (²) is coarser than the study grid (161²).
  • Ideal thermochemistry and nozzle. Frozen \(c^*\) and γ, no two-phase, divergence, boundary-layer or combustion-efficiency losses, and no flow-separation model: over-expanded thrust is clipped at zero rather than modelled. Delivered \(I_{sp}\) of real motors, especially KNSB, is lower.
  • Inputs are approximate. The propellant data are recalled public values, and the Monte Carlo tolerances are assumed and independent. Real burn-rate data carry correlated a–n uncertainty, temperature sensitivity \(\pi_K\), and batch effects, which should be added before any MEOP is used on hardware.
  • Ignition. The whole surface ignites at t = 0 with no igniter mass or flame-spread delay, so the start-up transient is a lower bound on the real rise time.
